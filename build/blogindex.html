Low Rank Adaptation of Language Models

Language models have a unique capability to adapt to domain-specific or task-specific data. But the cost of fully fine-tuning and deploying an independent copy of a large model for every task is rarely worth it. LoRA instead injects small, trainable rank-decomposition matrices into an otherwise frozen model, adapting behaviour without touching the original weights. These forms of methods can have tremendous safety implications

Essay

Alignment of Language Models

Jul 19, 2025 · Mukul Namagiri

Alignment is the process of ensuring a model's behaviour, outputs, and responses stay consistent with human values and goals.

We check a model's alignment through techniques like reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), adversarial testing, and interpretability methods. Given the current pace of progress in autonomous LLM capabilities, there's a strong case for investing early in more robust, more tractable, more interpretable systems.

"In a sensibly organized society, if you improve productivity, there is room for everybody to benefit. The problem isn't the technology, but the way the benefits are shared out." — Geoffrey Hinton

Universal standards for what counts as "safe" are hard to pin down in a field moving this fast — which is exactly why standardized evaluation methods matter so much.

AI Safety Criteria

  • Threat evaluation — can the model recognize when it's being misused?
  • Interpretability — can we trace why it did what it did?
  • Robustness — does it hold up under adversarial pressure?
  • Scalability — do the safeguards still work as capability grows?
  • Deception — is the model's behaviour honest about its own reasoning?

Essay

Adversarial Alignment

Dec 23, 2020 · Mukul Namagiri

Adversarial alignment tests whether a model can defend itself against toxicity, hate speech, and profanity, even when a user is actively trying to provoke it. Given how much pre-training data is scraped from unfiltered corners of the internet, some amount of harmful signal in a model's responses should be treated as the default risk, not the exception.

Attacks generally fall into two shapes: first-person attacks, where someone has direct, hands-on access and attacks the model through prompting, and embedded attacks, where a harmful instruction is hidden inside otherwise ordinary content that an unsuspecting user then feeds into the system.

Catching either kind requires monitoring beyond the model's outputs. Looking directly at model weights can surface deceptive or sycophantic behaviour before it ever shows up in a response, and it's a big part of preparing a model for the distribution shifts it will eventually meet in the wild.

Essay

Adversarial Robustness

Adversarial robustness is a model's ability to keep performing well when it's facing malicious or carefully crafted inputs, rather than the clean data it was mostly trained and tested on.

Attack Vectors

  • Direct model attacks: manipulation through adversarial prompts or inputs
  • Indirect attacks: exploiting system-level vulnerabilities or naive users

Defensive Measures

  • Input sanitization and validation
  • Adversarial training techniques
  • Model monitoring and anomaly detection

Technique

Prompt Tuning Using Propagation

Jun 2025 · Factity AI

"Prompt tuning" adapts a frozen language model to a specific task without touching its core parameters — a lighter-weight cousin of the fine-tuning approaches above.

Key Advantages

  • Soft prompts: unlike discrete text prompts, continuous "soft prompts" are learned through backpropagation and can absorb signal from labeled examples
  • Scalability: the approach becomes increasingly competitive with full model tuning as models grow past billions of parameters
  • Efficiency: one frozen model can be reused across many downstream tasks, cutting the compute cost of adaptation

Notable Findings

  • Outperforms few-shot prompting on the same base model
  • Simplifies earlier "prefix tuning" approaches while keeping their effectiveness
  • Shows improved robustness when transferred across domains
  • Supports efficient "prompt ensembling" for further gains

The net effect: adapting a large model to a new task gets dramatically cheaper, without giving up much of its underlying capability.
